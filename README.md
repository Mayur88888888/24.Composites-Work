Over the last few weeks, I have been developing an industrial engineering software platform focused specifically on compression molding of composite materials.
(Not yet published - keep watching on https://github.com/Mayur88888888 ).

The objective of this platform is not only to visualize a molding cycle, but to help engineers, students, researchers, and process developers understand the deep relationship between:
1.	Resin behavior
2.	Thermal energy transfer
3.	Compression pressure
4.	Cure kinetics
5.	Fiber reinforcement systems
6.	Process timing
7.	Material flow behavior
8.	Tool heating characteristics
9.	Composite part formation
Modern composite manufacturing is highly interconnected. 
A small variation in one process parameter can significantly influence the final part quality, cycle time, dimensional stability, and cure consistency.
My Tool attempts to digitally represent these interactions in a simplified but engineering-oriented environment.

Why Compression Molding?
Compression molding remains one of the most important manufacturing routes for:
1.	SMC (Sheet Molding Compound)
2.	DMC/BMC materials
3.	Carbon fiber thermosets
4.	Glass fiber reinforced composites
5.	High-volume structural composites
6.	Automotive lightweight components
7.	Electrical insulation products
8.	Aerospace secondary structures
Despite its widespread industrial use, many engineers still rely heavily on trial-and-error approaches during process setup.
I wanted to create a platform that visually explains why a process behaves the way it does.
Core Engineering Parameters Included
The software currently focuses on the critical engineering variables that dominate compression molding performance.
1.	Resin Parameters
2.	The resin system governs: Viscosity behavior, Thermal response, Cure progression, Reaction kinetics, Flow resistance, Crosslink density
Different resin families react differently under temperature and pressure.

2. Heater Power & Thermal Input
Heater wattage directly affects:
1.	Mold temperature rise
2.	Thermal uniformity
3.	Cure initiation
4.	Cycle stability
5.	Energy consumption
Higher heater power may reduce heating time, but excessive thermal gradients can create:
1.	Uneven curing
2.	Internal stresses
3.	Resin degradation
4.	Localized overheating
The software visualizes how thermal energy propagates through the molding process.
3. Compression Pressure
Pressure is one of the most influential variables in composite consolidation.
Compression pressure controls:
1.	Fiber wet-out
2.	Void reduction
3.	Laminate compaction
4.	Surface finish
5.	Thickness uniformity
6.	Resin redistribution
7.	Insufficient pressure can trap air and create porosity.
8.	Excessive pressure can force resin starvation or fiber distortion.
9.	The simulation framework attempts to demonstrate these balance points visually.

4. Cure Progression & Reaction Kinetics
Thermoset systems evolve continuously during processing.
The software models:
1.	Degree of cure
2.	Gelation behavior
3.	Cure stage progression
4.	Reaction advancement
5.	Thermal cure response
These parameters are deeply linked to:
1.	Mold temperature
2.	Heating rate
3.	Resin chemistry
4.	Hold time
Understanding cure progression is essential for achieving repeatable part quality.

5. Resin Flow Characteristics
One of the most challenging aspects of compression molding is predicting material movement inside the cavity.
The platform includes simplified resin flow behavior related to:
1.	Viscosity evolution
2.	fiber interaction
3.	Pressure distribution
4.	Cavity filling tendency
5.	Shear influence
6.	Thermal softening effects
Future developments aim to further enhance digital flow visualization.
Why I Built This
The purpose of this project is educational, industrial, and exploratory.
I observed that many advanced composite manufacturing concepts remain hidden behind expensive enterprise software ecosystems.
Our goal is to create a platform that:
1.	Visually explains composite process science
2.	Bridges simulation and manufacturing understanding
3.	Supports learning and experimentation
4.	Encourages process optimization thinking
5.	Makes engineering concepts more accessible
This is not intended to replace high-end industrial CAE platforms, but rather to create an intuitive engineering environment focused on process understanding.
